# Initial production baseline

## Public Summary

The NFL expected-points model predicts each team's score from recent team efficiency, quarterback play, game context, and sportsbook information. It converts those scores into spread and total picks, then uses separate confidence models to identify the predictions most likely to beat the available line.

## Changes

- Builds leakage-safe, lagged team form from dynamically windowed exponential averages of offensive and defensive EPA and custom success rate, including passing and rushing splits.
- Adds starting-quarterback passer rating, rest, divisional-game status, venue and weekday context, implied team points, and current betting inputs to the score model.
- Uses a gradient-boosted score model to predict each team's expected points, then derives the projected spread and total from those scores.
- Trains independent spread and total confidence classifiers only on genuine out-of-time score predictions. A market can become a lock when its estimated win probability is above 55%, its model edge is at least 0.5 points, and it ranks among the five strongest plays for that market.
- Refuses to create a pick at or after kickoff, preserves picks once they enter the 30-minute lock window, and atomically records update history, current picks, and newly graded results.

## Evaluation

Completed games are ordered chronologically. The newest 20% form an outer score holdout, score parameters are selected with one train-before-validation split inside the older data, and the production score model is refit on all completed games. The confidence models train from the outer out-of-time score predictions, tune on their own chronological split, and then refit on that full holdout.

## Internal Notes

Version 1.0 is the bootstrap baseline introduced after the model was already operating. Pre-versioning picks, results, and update runs are attributed to 1.0 because their exact recipe version and source Git SHA were not recorded. This document describes the production recipe in place when version tracking began; it does not claim that every historical row attributed to 1.0 was generated by an identical code revision.

Player-level inputs are limited to starting-quarterback passer rating, and team efficiency is not explicitly adjusted for opponent strength in this baseline.
